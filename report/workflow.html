Stage 5: the human comparison, specified

Status

This stage has not been run. No human-scored observations exist in the study data — the transcripts carry audio and metadata only, and the warehouse table that might hold coaching scores was not reachable. Every reliability figure produced so far describes whether the scorer agrees with itself. Nothing so far speaks to whether it is right.

What it needs

  • The same observations, scored by a trained human against the same rubric at the same proficiency cut. Re-using observations already scored by the AI costs no extra model calls.
  • Two or more human raters, ideally. Without a human–human figure there is nothing to judge the human–AI figure against: if two trained coaches only agree with each other 70% of the time, an AI agreeing with them 70% of the time is performing at human level, not failing.
  • Around 20–30 observations. Fewer will not separate a real gap from sampling noise on a per-indicator basis.

Which statistics, and why those

MeasureForWhy this one
Gwet's AC1met / not-met verdicts Same reason as in stage 3: with a lopsided split, kappa collapses toward zero at high agreement. AC1 does not.
Quadratic-weighted kappathe 1–4 level An ordinal scale should not treat a 3-vs-4 disagreement as equal to 1-vs-4. Weighting gives partial credit for adjacency.
Exact + adjacent agreementboth The plain number a non-statistician can check: how often identical, how often within one level.
ICC(2,1)section scores Section fidelity is continuous, so a correlation-style coefficient fits it better than a categorical one.
Bland–Altman biassection scores Separates disagreement from systematic leniency. An AI that is consistently one level harsher is a calibration problem, not a reliability one — and it is fixable.
Read the result against the ceiling

The AI agrees with itself at AC1 0.87. It cannot agree with a human better than that. So a human–AI figure of, say, 0.75 is not "0.25 short of perfect" — it is close to the maximum the scorer's own consistency allows.

This is also why the seven unreliable indicators should be excluded from the human comparison, or reported separately. An indicator whose verdict changes between two AI runs cannot meaningfully be compared to a human's single verdict — you would be measuring the noise, not the disagreement.
